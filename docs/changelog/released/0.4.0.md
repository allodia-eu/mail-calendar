## Changes in this release

Engineering commentary, carried over from each fragment. Never pasted into a store.

### Removing an account is somewhere you would look for it — `removing-an-account` (macos, ios, minor)

> The only route to removing an account was the mailbox sidebar's per-account context menu — a long
> press on a row that gives no sign it holds one. That is a poor place to keep the only copy of
> "remove my account", and it is what App Review asked about. Settings → Accounts now carries a
> **Remove account…** button at the foot of each account's card, below the settings you might
> actually adjust. It is a second route, not a replacement: the context menu keeps its own. Both go
> through the shell's `removeAccount` rather than the model directly, because removal is more than
> the model call — the shell also clears the scene's stored selection and any open message belonging
> to the account going away, and two entry points must not mean two behaviours. Android and Windows
> still have only the context menu; that gap is recorded in `docs/settings.md` and tracked in #211.

### Account setup is laid out for the screen it is on — `account-setup-fits-the-screen` (ios, patch)

> Setup is the screen right after the welcome, and it did not look like it: the welcome is a
> centred, width-capped column, while setup pinned itself top-leading, so on an 11-inch iPad it sat
> crushed into the corner with most of the display empty beside it. The manual form was worse — its
> fields were a SwiftUI `Form` nested inside a `VStack`, which is an inset-grouped list that claims
> every available point of height and insets its own rows, so it drew a ~1,400pt grey slab around
> ~500pt of fields, put the title, the card and the rows on three different left edges, and stranded
> the "just the server name" footnote a screen-height below the fields it explains. Neither root
> could scroll, so tall content simply clipped. Both roots now share one scaffold — they each
> carried a comment claiming they "must lay out identically", with nothing enforcing it — fields sit
> in the same `GroupBox` card the welcome screen uses, and the two emoji section headings (`✉`,
> `📅`) became SF Symbols.

### Attach files opens the picker again on iPhone and iPad — `attach-files-from-the-composer` (ios, patch)

> The composer's "Attach files" button did nothing at all on iOS and iPadOS — App Review rejected
> the build for it ("tapped 'attach file' but there was no response"). The picker was presented
> from the key window's **root** view controller, but on iOS the composer is a `fullScreenCover`,
> so the root was already presenting; UIKit refuses a second `present` on a busy controller
> **silently**, with no error and no callback, which is why it looked like a dead button rather
> than a failure. The share sheet next to it already walked the presentation chain to its top and
> carried a comment saying why — the file picker simply never got the same treatment. That walk is
> now one shared helper both call, and it is a plain function over a small protocol rather than
> UIKit, because `swift test` runs on macOS: an `#if os(iOS)` test of it would have compiled to
> nothing and reported a pass over the code that broke.

### A rotated sign-in is saved from the first second the app can refresh one — `credential-store-at-construction` (macos, ios, windows, android, patch)

> The app hands its OS secure store to the core when it builds it, instead of installing it a
> moment later. The old ordering was a race the app usually won: the constructor starts dialing
> every account in its last statement, and a production launch measured the first OAuth refresh
> beginning 6 ms later — while the host was still blocked inside the call and had run no code.
> Android and Windows then registered their store from a UI-thread post, so a Microsoft rotation
> arriving ~660 ms afterwards was saved or lost depending on whether the main thread had had a
> turn during app start. Losing one is silent for an hour and then permanent: the stored token is
> a generation behind the server's, and a provider that treats a replayed refresh token as theft
> (Fastmail) revokes the whole grant rather than just refusing it — which is how a real account
> died. The three provider-shaped store ports collapse into one, since all nine client
> implementations were the same single line and three ports is what made forgetting one cheap.

### The day preview on an invitation is readable — `invitation-day-preview-zoom` (macos, ios, windows, android, patch)

> The preview's hour band used to span the whole day's blocks. A normal working day runs 08:00–22:00,
> so fourteen hours were squeezed into the card's box, an hour came out around ten points, and every
> client's "is this block tall enough for a title" rule said no — including for the meeting the card
> is *about*, which drew as an unnamed rectangle beside a named one. Growing the box instead was
> tried and is worse: it pushes the message itself off the screen.
>
> The band is now the meeting, everything overlapping it, and an hour of air. Nothing the card counts
> can fall outside it (a conflict overlaps the meeting by definition, so it widens the band and is
> drawn whole); what is dropped is non-clashing context, and the disclosure label changed from "Your
> calendar that day" to "Around this meeting" rather than leaving the picture claiming more than it
> shows. Apple's preview also stopped drawing a block's second start-time line, matching Android and
> Windows — it was the one part of a scrolled-off block low enough to survive, so a bare "11:00" was
> floating under the all-day bar with no title above it.

### Every confirmation on iPad has a way out again — `ipad-confirmations-keep-their-cancel` (ios, patch)

> Seven confirmation prompts had no Cancel on iPad — removing an account, deleting an event,
> deleting a signature, discarding a draft, resetting the local database (two entry points) and
> sharing the diagnostic log. Each was a SwiftUI `confirmationDialog`, which iPadOS presents as a
> **popover**, and a popover drops the `.cancel`-role button on the reasoning that tapping outside
> dismisses it. So a destructive prompt rendered as a single red button. The Cancel was in the code
> the whole time and could not be shown; the discard-draft one lost "Keep editing", which is the
> answer most people want.
>
> All seven are now `alert`s, which show every button on every platform and size class — and which
> is what Android (`AlertDialog` + `dismissButton`) and Windows (`ContentDialog` + `CloseButtonText`)
> already do, so Apple was the odd one out rather than this being a new pattern. Found because App
> Review asked where a user removes their account; the other six were the same defect standing next
> to it.

### The MCP relay moved, so its config snippet must be copied again — `mcp-relay-moved-into-a-bundle` (macos, patch)

> Preparing the Mac App Store build turned up a hard constraint: the Store rejects any upload whose
> nested executables are not sandboxed (ITMS-90296), and a *bare* executable cannot be sandboxed at
> all — with no bundle identifier there is no container to attach, so it dies in `libsecinit`
> before `main()`. The relay is therefore a nested `.app`
> (`Contents/Library/Helpers/allodia-mcp.app`) on both macOS flows, rather than two layouts.
>
> That changes the path an assistant spawns, so a config pasted from an older build points at a
> file that is no longer there — the assistant reports only "failed to spawn process", naming
> nobody. Hence a user-facing note for what is otherwise a packaging change: the fix is to copy the
> snippet from Settings → Advanced again.

### Switching accounts in the Windows sidebar is instant again — `windows-sidebar-switching` (windows, patch)

> The sidebar was rebuilt by hand — `NavigationView.MenuItems.Clear()` and a fresh
> `NavigationViewItem` tree — once per `CollectionChanged` event, and the model refills its `Folders`
> collection with `Clear()` plus one `Add()` per folder. So opening an account with N folders cost
> N+2 complete rebuilds, each itself O(N). Measured against a real 57-folder account: sixty rebuilds
> and **10.1 seconds** of frozen UI thread, **16.1 s** on the second visit because the discarded
> trees were retained and every later rebuild grew dearer. 329 rebuilds and 39.7 s of UI thread went
> by in one three-minute session. The core's half of the same refresh takes 2 ms — this was never the
> engine.
>
> The accordion is bound to a collection now (`ViewModels/SidebarTree.cs`) and reconciled in place,
> so the framework hears only what actually moved and an unchanged row keeps its container. The same
> switch is **53–62 ms**, flat across repeat visits; picking a folder inside the open account is
> 7–11 ms, because the folder set does not change and nothing is raised at all.
>
> Two things fell out of it. The pane-collapse crash (`COMException` from
> `NavigationView.IsPaneOpen`, arriving as a stowed exception in `Microsoft.UI.Xaml.dll`) was the
> churn degrading the control until its own property setter failed — reproducible at ~316 rebuilds,
> gone with them. And unhandled exceptions are now written to the diagnostic log: that crash's only
> record anywhere was a Windows Error Reporting entry with an offset in `combase.dll`, while
> `app.log` simply stopped, which reads exactly like a clean exit.
>
> Two traps worth knowing for anyone touching this XAML. `x:Bind` cannot be used in a
> NavigationView's item template — it compiles a cast to `x:DataType` and the control hands the
> template a raw `WinRT.IInspectable`, so every row throws before the sidebar draws. And a
> NavigationView applies that template to its **footer** items too: the Contacts and Calendar entries
> were silently re-templated and lost their `AutomationProperties.AutomationId` while looking
> completely correct on screen. The uitests caught the second one; nothing else would have.

### The Windows title bar follows the app's theme — `windows-title-bar` (windows, patch)

> The window's caption was the *system* one, which is a separate surface from the app's content and
> does not read its theme: on a dark-mode desktop the app came up dark with a pale strip across the
> top of it. The only lever the system caption offers is a handful of `AppWindowTitleBar` colour
> properties that then have to be re-set by hand on every theme change — so the fix is to stop using
> it. The window now extends its content into the caption and draws a WinUI `TitleBar` control there
> ([Microsoft's guidance](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/develop/ui/controls/title-bar)),
> which is content like everything else and therefore inherits `ActualTheme` for free, and which owns
> the drag regions, the caption-button spacing and the min-drag region so none of that is
> hand-computed. It carries the app icon and title, and the NavigationView's pane toggle moves up
> beside them (the Fluent guidance when a custom title bar exists) — hidden on the welcome and setup
> screens, where there is no pane behind it to collapse. The window also gains a Mica backdrop, so
> the caption and the sidebar read as one surface rather than a strip painted over the app.
