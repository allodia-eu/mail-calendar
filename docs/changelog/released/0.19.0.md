## macos

**English**

```
The calendar remembers the day you are looking at: switching between week and month keeps your place, and the selected day is marked.

On a wide screen, a small month beside the calendar moves it to any day, and every calendar can be shown, hidden or recoloured where it is listed, with Mail, Calendar, Contacts and Settings pinned underneath.

Messages sent with high or low importance are now marked in the list and say so when opened, and you can set a message’s importance while writing it.

Resetting the local database now keeps Settings open and says whether it worked, and AI assistant access says when another copy of the app is already serving assistants.

While the calendar loads a period, its grid stays in view with a spinner over it.

Moving to the next or previous week or month in the calendar is instant again.

Calendar weeks and months more than four months back or a year ahead now load, instead of showing "Loading this period…".

The default calendar and video meeting settings now keep up as calendars arrive or change, and a calendar list with one account no longer repeats its address.

The attach button now shows that it is working while files you chose, such as a video from your photo library, are still being prepared.

Answering a meeting invitation addressed to several people now replies from your own address, so the answer no longer stays in the Outbox.

Printing a message works again in the version from the Mac App Store, which answered that the application does not support printing.

In a narrow window, a long sender name no longer pushes the message you are writing off the edges of its pane.

When you invite people to an event, the address suggestions no longer run under Cancel and Save.

Quoted text in messages from Thunderbird now wraps to the width of the reading pane instead of running off its edge.

A new message is never written from an account used only for its calendar or contacts, and Settings offers a default send account only when there is more than one to choose from.

A mail server given as an IPv6 address, such as [::1]:993, is now accepted in setup and on an account's page.

Changing a setting no longer waits on saving it, and if a setting can’t be saved you are told and can try again.

For an email address on a domain of your own, signing in through your provider from an account’s settings now uses the same sign-in server as adding the account.

Saving a signature now shows that it is working and cannot add it twice, and its pictures are PNG, JPEG or GIF up to 512 KB on every device, so every recipient sees them.

A message you open and leave untouched, including a reply or a draft from another app, is no longer saved to Drafts on its own.
```

## Changes in this release

Engineering commentary, carried over from each fragment. Never pasted into a store.

### The calendar keeps one day in focus across its views — `calendar-focus-date` (macos, ios, windows, android, linux, minor)

> A product decision for every client (`docs/calendar.md`, "The focus date"): the calendar keeps one
> focus date, held by the core, and every view shows the period around it. Switching from the week
> to the month and back returns to the same week; paging the month moves the focus by a month on the
> same day, so the week opened from there is the week of that day; a step into the week or month
> holding today lands on today. The month and the mini month ring the focus, and a press on a day of
> the month moves it there. Before, each client chose its own week after a paged month: the 1st's
> week (Linux, Android, Apple) or this week (Windows).

### The calendar has a pane of its own — `calendar-pane` (macos, ios, windows, linux, minor)

> On a desktop, and on a tablet at full width, the pane beside the calendar shows a mini month and
> every calendar under its account in place of the folder tree, with Mail, Calendar, Contacts and
> Settings pinned beneath. The calendar manager dialog is gone there; a phone keeps it.

### Message importance: read it, and send it — `message-importance` (macos, ios, windows, android, linux, minor)

> A high or low importance a sender set (`Importance`, or `X-Priority`) is now read on every
> account: the list row marks it, a conversation row is high when any message in it is, and an
> open message carries a banner. The composer's new Importance menu sends it, and a stored draft
> keeps it (docs/importance.md).

### Settings → Advanced says what a reset and assistant access came to — `advanced-in-core` (macos, ios, windows, android, linux, patch)

> Resetting the local database keeps Settings open: the button shows it is working until mail is
> downloaded again, then says whether the reset worked, and a second press waits for the first.
> On a desktop, AI assistant access says when it is starting and when another copy of the app is
> already serving assistants, instead of reading as running.

### The Android calendar's week opens on its first day after the month — `android-calendar-week-after-month` (android, patch)

> Choosing a week, work week or day from the view menu while on the month seated the week on the
> 1st of the month, so July 2026 put a Wednesday under the first heading; the same after the month
> and then the agenda. And a calendar reopened on the month in the first days of a month whose first
> week began in the month before showed that earlier month. The pager now seats every week through
> the core's week start, and seeds the month with today.

### The calendar keeps its grid while a period loads — `calendar-loading-spinner` (macos, ios, windows, android, linux, patch)

> A week, day or month the app is still fetching keeps its grid, with a spinner and "Loading this
> period…" floating over it where it can be seen. On Linux the week used to go blank and put the
> words above the hours on screen, so they were found only by scrolling up to midnight; the other
> clients now show the same spinner (`docs/calendar.md`, "Showing a period that is loading").

### Paging the calendar no longer pauses — `calendar-month-page-once` (macos, ios, windows, android, linux, patch)

> The core laid out a month page by placing every event on the day axis once for each of the 42
> cells, a time-zone conversion every time. On a calendar of 1,600 occurrences that took half a
> second a page, and Linux paid it on every week it paged, because it keeps the month current
> beside the week. Each occurrence is now placed once, and only those inside the six weeks are kept
> (`crates/mailcal-viewmodel/src/calendar/month.rs`): measured on Linux, a week step went from
> 500 to 830 ms to 30 to 50 ms in a debug build.

### A calendar page more than a few months back or a year ahead loads — `calendar-page-outside-horizon` (macos, ios, windows, android, linux, patch)

> The calendar expanded a fixed window around today, and a page outside it said "Loading this
> period…" for good. Asking for such a page now widens the window to it for the session, and on a
> Google or Microsoft account first fetches the events there, which that account's first sync never
> asked its server for. The user-facing line is unchanged: the page loads, now with every event.

### Calendar and video meeting settings keep up with the calendars — `calendar-settings-in-core` (macos, ios, windows, android, linux, patch)

> Settings → Calendar's default calendar and Settings → Videoconferencing are drawn from the
> core's records and follow its calendar signals, so they no longer show the calendars as they
> were when Settings opened (Linux, Windows). Every calendar picker names an account only while
> there are two or more.

### Attach shows that chosen files are still on their way — `composer-attach-shows-copying` (macos, ios, windows, android, linux, patch)

> The composer's Attach is a control the core gives a state: it runs from the moment files the
> person chose are on their way until they are on the message, which on iPhone and iPad covers a
> film from the photo library being converted after its picker has closed.

### Answering an invitation answers as you, not as another attendee — `invitation-reply-sender` (macos, ios, windows, android, linux, patch)

> An invitation addressed to several attendees, as Exchange sends one, was sometimes answered as the
> first of them rather than as you, so the reply stayed in the Outbox when the mail server refused
> that sender. The answer now goes out from your own address, or from the alias the invitation was
> delivered to.

### Linux frees what a closed composer, window or signature editor held, and a new composer always draws — `linux-composer-memory` (linux, patch)

> Every composer opened, message window closed, signature edited and message printed kept a
> process of its own running afterwards, about 180 MB each. Once enough had piled up, a new
> composer could open with an empty space where the message belongs.

### Linux's manual setup no longer says it is still checking — `linux-manual-setup-checking` (linux, patch)

> The manual account form kept "Checking how you can sign in…" on screen after a server that
> takes a password had answered, so it read as still waiting. The form now says nothing while the
> server is asked, and only what an answer adds.

### The reading spinner keeps its size on Linux — `linux-reading-spinner-size` (linux, patch)

> The reading pane's loading spinner was a stack page of its own, so it was allocated, and drawn,
> as large as the whole body area. It is now 32 px and centred.

### Printing works in the Mac App Store build — `mac-app-store-printing` (macos, patch)

> The Store build is the only sandboxed one and did not grant `com.apple.security.print`, so AppKit
> refused the print sheet with "This application does not support printing". The grant is added,
> and `cargo xtask check-store-sandbox` now pairs the print call with it.

### A long sender no longer pushes the Mac composer off its pane — `mac-composer-long-sender` (macos, patch)

> The From menu was as wide as its longest account, so with a long sender name and a narrow
> window the composer ran past both edges of its pane. It now fits the pane and shortens the
> sender instead.

### An event's invitee suggestions stay inside the editor on a Mac — `mac-invitee-suggestions-inside-sheet` (macos, patch)

> The suggestion list was hung under the input with no limit on its height, so under the invitee
> field, at the foot of the event editor, it ran past the form and under the sheet's bar: the bar's
> divider crossed the list and Cancel was drawn over it. The Mac now places the list as the phone
> does, below the input while there is room for about three suggestions and above it otherwise,
> never taller than that room.

### A Thunderbird quote wraps in the reading pane — `reading-wraps-thunderbird-quotes` (macos, ios, windows, android, linux, patch)

> Thunderbird quotes a plain-text message as `<pre wrap>`. The sanitiser dropped `wrap`, so the
> quote's long lines ran off the pane's right edge; it now keeps the attribute, and the base sheet
> lays a `pre[wrap]` out with `white-space: pre-wrap`, as Gecko does. A plain `<pre>` is unchanged.

### A new message comes from an account used for mail — `send-account-in-core` (macos, ios, windows, android, linux, patch)

> The account a new message is written from, and the default send account in Settings →
> Composing, now consider only accounts used for mail, so an account kept for its calendar or
> contacts alone is never the sender. Settings offers a choice only when there are two or more
> such accounts, on Linux as everywhere else.

### An IPv6 address works as a mail server — `server-address-ipv6` (macos, ios, windows, android, linux, patch)

> A mail server given as an IPv6 address, bare (`::1`) or in brackets with a port (`[::1]:993`),
> is accepted in setup and on an account's page, where it used to read as no server at all. The
> core splits and joins every server's host and port, so each form shows the port it dials.

### Settings are saved without holding up the window, and a failed save is said — `settings-saved-behind` (macos, ios, windows, android, linux, patch)

> Every preference is applied by the core at once and written to the preferences file in the
> background, so no control waits on the disk. A write that fails raises a notice with Try again
> instead of being dropped, and the calendar's default zoom offers the same choices on every
> platform.

### Signing in from an account's settings at the server setup found — `sign-in-issuer-dns` (macos, ios, windows, android, linux, patch)

> Setup finds a provider hosting the address's domain through its MX record, which needs the host's
> DNS. The sign-in an IMAP account's settings offer ran the same detection without it, so for such
> a domain it named another authorization server, or none (seen with Soverin: `my.soverin.net` at
> setup, `soverin.net` from Settings). It now looks through the DNS every client hands the core at
> launch, and an answer given before that is not kept.

### Signatures are saved by the core, once, and take the pictures every mail app shows — `signatures-in-core` (macos, ios, windows, android, linux, patch)

> Settings → Signatures is drawn from one core record. Save and Delete run off the UI thread
> with a spinner until the library is written, and a double press on Save no longer creates two
> signatures. The name and picture rules are the core's: PNG, JPEG or GIF up to 512 KB, so Android
> and Apple no longer accept SVG or HEIC files, and the file pickers offer only those three. A
> library that could not be written raises the failed-save notice instead of being dropped.

### A composer nobody wrote in is no longer saved to Drafts — `untouched-composer-saves-nothing` (macos, ios, windows, android, linux, patch)

> The autosave measures edits against the editor's change count as the opening seeds left it,
> read in the batch that snapshots the seeded document. Apple read it as the view appeared, before
> the seeds, so a blank New Message was stored as an empty draft and a reopened Thunderbird or
> Outlook draft was saved over in this app's markup; Android and Linux took it on the first sample,
> which missed a short reply typed before it and, on a slow load, could count the seeds. Windows
> counted the Subject's own opening value, which WinUI reports a turn later, as an edit.

### The calendar's arrows count every press on Windows — `windows-calendar-arrows-count-every-press` (windows, patch)

> A press of the calendar's previous or next arrow while the grid was still sliding from the last
> one stepped from wherever the slide had got to, so quick presses travelled less than asked and
> the grid could rest part of the way into a day.

### The Windows calendar's header names the week after the month — `windows-calendar-header-after-month` (windows, patch)

> Paging the month and then choosing a week, work week or day from the view menu left the month's
> name in the header whenever the grid returned to the days it last showed: "July 2026" over a
> week in November.

### The Windows calendar's week opens on its first day after the agenda or the month — `windows-calendar-week-after-agenda` (windows, patch)

> A calendar last left on the agenda or the month seated the week, from the view menu or Today, on
> today rather than on the week's first day, so a Tuesday stood under the first heading.

### New-mail notifications on the installed Windows app — `windows-store-new-mail-notifications` (windows, patch)

> A packaged app's `AppNotificationManager.Register()` looks for the COM server its manifest
> declares with the runtime's activation argument, and the manifest declared none, so every
> Store and direct-download build logged `notifications: could not register (COMException
> 0x80004005)` and raised no toast. The manifest now declares the toast activator and its COM
> server, under a CLSID derived from the package identity so the Store and direct packages, which
> can be installed together, do not share one. The UI suites run the unpackaged build, which
> registers its server at runtime, so they never saw it; `test_msix_manifest` now does.
