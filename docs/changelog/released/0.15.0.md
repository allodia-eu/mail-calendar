## macos

**English**

```
A conversation's menu now offers what a message's does, including reply, flag and report as junk; a flagged conversation shows its flag, and a conversation folds away once you open something else.

Mailboxes shared with your account, such as a team's, including on Microsoft 365, can be added in Settings → Accounts. Each appears as a mailbox of its own and sends from its own address.

An event's video meeting, from Teams, Meet, Zoom and others, can be joined from its detail with one tap, beside its meeting ID, passcode and dial-in numbers, and the calendar marks the event with a camera.

Link a phone number so that tapping it starts a call, in a message or in your signature.

Marking a whole conversation read now touches only the messages you received, flagging it flags the newest of those, and archiving or deleting it leaves your drafts in place.

Gmail accounts sync faster: a mailbox of a thousand messages now arrives in seconds rather than minutes.

A Gmail account’s page in Settings now shows its sync technology, Optimised or Fallback, and says when a network keeps its mail from arriving.

A draft opens in its composer wherever you find it, such as inside a conversation in your inbox, not only in Drafts.

Photos of people in your organisation directory and in other contact folders now appear.

When a server has no calendar or address book for your account, its page now says so instead of reporting the server as unreachable.

Calendar and contacts servers such as Nextcloud now connect, still encrypted, when their address redirects to an unencrypted one, and a server address with a path is used as you entered it.

Signing in with Google on a computer no longer fails after you have approved it in the browser.

A Gmail account’s folder list no longer shows its inbox tabs, chat and extra stars as folders with names like CATEGORY_SOCIAL; that mail is still in the Inbox or All Mail.

Mail accounts sign in to their servers far less often on a device whose network status keeps changing, so servers no longer limit them for it.

A Gmail account’s folder list shows All Mail once, and Important in your language rather than as IMPORTANT.

Saving a draft with attachments writes much less to disk, and a draft or message is no longer kept on the device a second time once the server has it.

After you archive or delete a message, the next one opens as it would from the list, so a draft opens for editing.

Removing an account now takes it off screen straight away; the space it used is freed afterwards, in the background.

Choosing to keep more mail while an account is still downloading now fetches the older mail too.
```

## Changes in this release

Engineering commentary, carried over from each fragment. Never pasted into a store.

### A conversation's menu, its flag and a draft opening anywhere on Android — `android-conversation-menu` (android, minor)

> Every row's ⋮ menu is drawn from the core's `row_menu`, a conversation row's included, and on
> a selected row it acts on the selection, so a conversation and a selection can be filed in a
> named folder. The selection bar reads its toggles and what responds from the same answer. A
> conversation row, and each message on its screen, shows its flag. A draft resumes in a composer
> wherever it is listed, told by the row rather than by the open folder, and tapping a
> conversation opens its newest message.

### Shared mailboxes on Android — `android-shared-mailboxes` (android, minor)

> The Android client gains the Shared mailboxes section on an account's page in Settings →
> Accounts and the "Also mailboxes shared with you" choice beneath mail in a Microsoft setup,
> drawing the core's shared-mailbox calls that Linux already uses.

### A conversation's menu on macOS, iPhone and iPad — `apple-conversation-menu` (macos, ios, minor)

> The row menus are drawn from the core's `row_menu`, which replaced four hand-built menus (a
> message, a conversation, a message inside one, a selection). The selection bar reads its toggles
> and what responds from the same answer. Opening a conversation goes to its newest message, and a
> conversation folds away once the selection leaves it. A conversation, and each message in it,
> shows its flag.

### Shared mailboxes on macOS and iOS — `apple-shared-mailboxes` (macos, ios, minor)

> The Apple client's Settings → Accounts section and Microsoft setup choice for shared mailboxes,
> as Linux ships them: a switch per mailbox the server lists, an address field on Microsoft 365,
> and "Also mailboxes shared with you" beneath mail in a Microsoft setup. The core, the scopes and
> the privacy policy are as the Linux note describes.

### Video meetings on macOS, iPhone and iPad — `apple-video-meetings` (macos, ios, minor)

> The event detail draws the core's `EventDetail.conferences`: Join first, then Copy link, the
> meeting ID, the passcode and the dial-in numbers, and shows `display_notes`, without the
> invitation's meeting block. Blocks, all-day bars, month chips and agenda rows draw a camera where
> `has_conference` is set, and say "video meeting" to VoiceOver.

### Telephone links in the composer — `composer-telephone-links` (macos, ios, windows, android, linux, minor)

> A link may now carry `tel:` as well as `http`, `https` and `mailto`, so a signature or a message can
> make a number dial when it is tapped. The scheme joins `mailcal_composer::LINK_SCHEMES`, which is
> the one allowlist behind the link dialog, the reading view's sanitiser, the linkifier and
> `should_open_external_link`, so all four moved together. A bare number is still refused rather than
> completed into `tel:` in the address field, and a `tel:` written as text is still not linked: a
> wrong guess dials a stranger. A `tel:` number typed with spaces has them dropped, since the URI
> may not hold any. A `tel:` link must be a number RFC 3966 can write, and one holding `*` or `#`
> (a service code, such as call forwarding) is neither sent, kept by the sanitiser nor opened.
> Nothing changes about which other schemes are accepted. Privacy policy 2.8.

### What an action on a whole conversation reaches — `conversation-action-reach` (macos, ios, windows, android, linux, minor)

> The selection bar and "Archive conversation" reach a conversation's messages through the table
> in docs/row-actions.md, which the row menu is labelled from too. Mark read used to touch every
> message, the owner's Sent copies included, and Flag flagged them all; both now reach what Outlook
> reaches. A conversation's archive, delete or move used to take its drafts with it.

### Gmail mail synced over IMAP — `gmail-over-imap` (macos, ios, windows, android, linux, minor)

> Gmail's API bills each message's details and body against a per-user quota that cannot be
> raised, which held a first sync to about 270 messages a minute. A Google account now reads its
> mail over IMAP with the same sign-in: 856 messages and their details in about 5 seconds rather
> than 8 minutes. Labels and every change still go through the API.

### Sync technology on a Gmail account's page — `gmail-sync-technology` (macos, ios, windows, android, linux, minor)

> A Gmail account reads its mail over IMAP where Gmail allows it and over the Gmail API where it
> does not, which is far slower. Settings → Accounts now says which, as Optimised or Fallback
> (Checking until the account's first read in this launch), with an info button that explains it
> and links to the help page on getting the fast way back. A network that blocks IMAP while the
> rest of Google answers stopped Gmail mail without a word; the page now says Can't connect and
> the account is badged as unreachable.

### A conversation's menu, its flag and a draft opening anywhere on Linux — `linux-conversation-menu` (linux, minor)

> The row menus are drawn from the core's `row_menu`, which replaced the hand-built menus (a
> message, a conversation, a message inside one); the selection bar reads its toggles and what
> responds from the same answer. Opening a conversation goes to its newest message, and a
> conversation folds away once the selection leaves it. A conversation, and each message in it,
> shows its flag. A draft resumes in a composer wherever it is listed, told by the row rather
> than by the open folder.

### Shared mailboxes on Linux — `linux-shared-mailboxes` (linux, minor)

> A mailbox shared with an IMAP or JMAP account (an RFC 2342 shared namespace, or a JMAP session's
> non-personal account) can be switched on from the account's page in Settings → Accounts. On
> Microsoft 365 it is a use of its own beneath mail, asking for `Mail.ReadWrite.Shared` and
> `Mail.Send.Shared` only while chosen, and a mailbox is added by its address, since Graph lists
> none. Each is listed as a mail account of its own, opened with the parent account's sign-in, and
> sends from its own address under the name its server holds. The Azure app registration needs
> both scopes as delegated permissions before this ships, or a sign-in asking for them is refused.
> The engine bump that brings this re-reads every account's folders once, so the first launch
> after updating syncs every account in full once, on every platform. Privacy policy 2.7.

### A conversation's menu, its flag and its draft on Windows — `windows-conversation-menu` (windows, minor)

> The row menus are drawn from the core's `row_menu`, which replaced the hand-built message menu
> and the conversation's two items, and a message inside an expanded conversation has a message's
> full menu. Mark as Spam and Mark as Not Spam reach Windows through it. The selection bar reads its

> message, and a conversation folds away once the selection leaves it. A conversation, and each
> message in it, shows its flag. A draft opens in its composer by the row's own `draft`, not by the
> open folder.

### Shared mailboxes on Windows — `windows-shared-mailboxes` (windows, minor)

> The Shared mailboxes section on an account's page in Settings → Accounts, the Microsoft address

### A long account address in the Android folder pane — `android-folder-pane-long-address` (android, patch)

> The folder pane's account row let a line break inside the address and kept only the first line,
> so an address with a hyphen showed only the part before it. It now stays on one line and is
> shortened in the middle.

### A draft opens in its composer wherever it is listed, on macOS, iPhone and iPad — `apple-draft-opens-anywhere` (macos, ios, patch)

> A row says whether its message is a draft (`FlatRow::draft`, `ThreadMessage::draft`), and the
> Apple client resumes a draft by that rather than by the open folder, so a draft inside a
> conversation in the Inbox, or in a search result, opens in its composer as it does in Drafts.

### Contact photos from every source of an account — `contact-photos-from-every-source` (macos, ios, windows, android, linux, patch)

> An account connects one contacts adapter per source, and every photo was asked of the first,
> which knows only its own ids. Photos of people in a Microsoft account's organisation
> directory or a second contact folder were refused and stayed initials.

### A calendar or address book the server does not have is said once — `dav-none-on-server` (macos, ios, windows, android, linux, patch)

> A DAV server that answered it has no calendar or address book for the account (a start that is
> gone, no home, or a 501 for a request it does not implement) was shown as unreachable and
> dialled again on every refresh. It now says the server has none for this account, and is
> dialled again only with the account.

### Calendar and contacts servers behind a TLS-terminating proxy — `dav-redirect-over-https` (macos, ios, windows, android, linux, patch)

> A server that redirects from HTTPS to `http://` on its own host, which Nextcloud's
> `.well-known` rewrite does behind a TLS-terminating proxy, is followed over HTTPS, both when
> setup looks for it and when the engine connects; a plaintext redirect to another host is still
> refused. A calendar or address book URL with a path now starts discovery at that path rather
> than at the host's `.well-known`, and goes back to the `.well-known` when the path is gone.
> Contacts that use the calendar's server still start at its CardDAV `.well-known`.

### Google sign-in on a desktop no longer fails when the browser asks for more than the redirect — `desktop-google-sign-in-redirect` (macos, windows, patch)

> The loopback listener took the first request that reached its port as the redirect, so a
> browser fetching `/favicon.ico` first failed a completed sign-in with "callback missing state".
> macOS and Windows now take only a request carrying `state` with a `code` or an `error`
> (`is_oauth_callback`, the rule Linux already applied, now shared), read the whole request first,
> and answer anything else with a 400.

### Gmail's inbox tabs, chat and extra stars are not folders — `gmail-unfiled-labels` (macos, ios, windows, android, linux, patch)

> Gmail lists its inbox tabs (`CATEGORY_*`), `CHAT` and its extra stars (`YELLOW_STAR`,
> `RED_BANG`, …) as labels, and the engine filed messages in them, so a Gmail account's folder
> tree drew each under its raw id. None is a place a user files mail in. The engine now leaves
> them out of the folder list and of every message's folders, the way it already treated read and
> starred. A message in a tab still carries the Inbox label; one archived from a tab is in All
> Mail. `NORMALIZER_VERSION` 12 has every account re-read its mail and calendars once on upgrade.

### A row that moves and changes is redrawn on Linux — `linux-moved-row-redraws` (linux, patch)

> The message list reconciles in place, and a row whose key moved position had its widget
> reparented without comparing its rendering, so it kept what it showed before. Leaving the Inbox
> for Drafts did this to a conversation holding a draft: it moved to the top of Drafts still
> showing the received message's subject and sender rather than the draft's. A moved row is now
> rebuilt when its rendering changed, as one that stayed in place already was.

### Fewer sign-ins when the network status flickers — `network-report-storm` (macos, ios, windows, android, linux, patch)

> macOS's network monitor reports the network as available every few seconds with nothing
> changed (measured: seven reports in ninety seconds on a Mac with VPN interfaces). The core took
> each as the network coming back, dropping every idle IMAP connection and dialling every
> unreachable calendar and address book again, so a mail server saw a sign-in storm and answered
> by limiting the account. Only a report that ends an offline spell does that now.

### One All Mail per account, and Important named in your language — `one-all-mail` (macos, ios, windows, android, linux, patch)

> A Gmail account's folder tree drew All Mail twice on Apple (the account's own and Gmail's), and
> Gmail's Important label under its id, `IMPORTANT`. A provider's all-mail folder is no longer
> drawn beside the account's own, and Important takes the app's word for it.

### Save drafts with attachments without writing them again at every step — `outbox-writes-attachments-once` (macos, ios, windows, android, linux, patch)

> The engine's outbox kept each op's payload in its row, so every claim and outcome rewrote it
> (a draft's payload carries its attachments), and kept it after the op settled: one store held
> 707 MB of settled payloads in 1.5 GB. The payload now lives beside the row, written once, and
> goes when the op settles (engine schema v20). The freed space is reused before the store grows
> and given back to the disk at the next compaction.

### The reading pane moves on to a draft as a click would — `reading-pane-moves-on-to-a-draft` (macos, ios, windows, linux, patch)

> After an archive or delete from the reading pane, the core now names the message the pane moves
> on to (`stop_after_removing`) and whether it is a draft, and each client opens it as clicking its
> row would. The three clients each worked the next message out for themselves, disagreed about
> which message a collapsed conversation stands for, and opened a draft read-only in the pane.

### Remove an account without waiting for the store to be compacted — `remove-account-without-waiting` (macos, ios, windows, android, linux, patch)

> Removing an account compacted the whole store (`VACUUM`) before the removal returned, and on
> Apple that call ran on the main thread: a 1.5 GB store held the window for about 17 seconds.
> The compaction now runs after the removal, unawaited, and Apple calls the removal off the main
> actor.

### Change how much mail to keep while an account is still downloading — `sync-depth-during-first-sync` (macos, ios, windows, android, linux, patch)

> A depth change cleared the mail cursors, but a pass still running under the old depth wrote
> its own back over the clear, so a long Gmail first sync went on under the old depth and the
> older mail the new one includes was never fetched. The change now stops that pass, and a
> cursor written under a narrower depth is never resumed by a wider one.
