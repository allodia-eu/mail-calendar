# Allodia Mail & Calendar 0.6.0

Released 28 August 2026.

Fifty-seven changes, and three of them are what this release is about. A repeating event now says exactly how it repeats, and changing or deleting a single occurrence asks whether you meant that one or the whole series instead of quietly taking the lot. There is an Allodia account of your own, and with it the mail accounts you add on one device are offered on the next — the addresses and server settings, never your mail and never your passwords. And mail arrives far faster: in our testing, adding a large Gmail account went from about nine minutes to under thirty seconds.

Changes are grouped by where they landed. The first section reached **every app**. The sections after it cover changes that did not reach all of them, so a change that landed on two apps is listed under both.

---

## 🌟 Every app: macOS, iPhone & iPad, Windows and Android

### ✨ New

- **A repeating event says exactly how it repeats.** A repeating event now says how it repeats — every second Tuesday, the fourth Monday of the month, until a date — instead of just "Repeats".
- **An Allodia account of your own.** Settings now has its own place to create an Allodia account, sign in, or delete it, separately from your mail accounts.
- **Changing a series warns before it undoes your changes.** Changing a whole repeating event now warns you first if your account's server would undo what you changed on a single occurrence, such as a time you moved or a name you gave it.
- **Changing one occurrence asks which ones you meant.** Editing one occurrence of a repeating event now asks whether you meant this event or all of them, instead of always changing the whole series.
- **Choose how big a message to keep offline.** Each account can now choose the largest message to download in the background — 2, 5 or 10 MB, or no limit. Raising it downloads what was skipped; lowering it frees space without removing any mail.
- **Deleting one occurrence no longer deletes them all.** Deleting one occurrence of a repeating event now asks whether you meant this event or all of them, instead of quietly removing the whole series.
- **Fastmail and Outlook download messages faster.** Fastmail, other JMAP accounts and Outlook now download message bodies several at a time — in our testing about nine times faster for Fastmail and three times for Outlook.
- **Gmail fills in much faster after you add it.** Adding a Gmail account no longer downloads its messages one after another: in our testing, a large account went from about nine minutes to under thirty seconds.
- **Phones skip very large messages, computers download them all.** On phones and tablets, very large messages now arrive when you open them instead of downloading in the background, which saves mobile data. Computers download every size, so all your mail reads offline.
- **Your accounts on every device.** With an Allodia account, the mail accounts you add on one device are offered on the next — the addresses and server settings, never your mail and never your passwords.

### 🔧 Fixed

- **A busy mail server no longer stops a sync.** When a mail server asks us to slow down, we now wait the time it asks for and carry on, instead of leaving that part of the sync unfinished.
- **A folder opens as soon as you pick it.** Choosing a folder now opens it straight away, and its mail fills in behind it.
- **A message opens without a spinner.** Moving between messages no longer flashes a loading spinner; it appears only when a message actually takes a moment to open.
- **A new event opens ready to type.** A new calendar event now opens with the cursor already in the title.
- **A new message opens ready to type.** A new message now opens with the cursor already in the To field.
- **A repeating event no longer claims the wrong rhythm.** An event that repeats every second week no longer says it repeats weekly; a rhythm we cannot describe in one word now reads as "Repeats".
- **A repeating event opens on the day you tapped.** Opening one occurrence of a repeating event now shows that occurrence's own date and time, instead of the date the series started on.
- **Calendar diagnostics hide account addresses.** Calendar diagnostics no longer include the account's email address.
- **Gmail keeps what is in Junk and Trash.** Messages in Junk and Trash on Gmail no longer disappear and reappear between syncs.
- **One appointment in a series can be changed.** Changing a single appointment in a repeating series now works on Google calendars, instead of being refused.
- **One odd appointment no longer stops your calendar.** One appointment with an unusual repeat rule no longer stops the rest of your calendar from arriving.
- **Your Allodia sign-in says when it needs renewing.** When your Allodia sign-in is older than a feature it needs, the app now says so and offers to sign you in again, instead of showing a technical error.

## 🖥️ macOS

### ✨ New

- **A new account on a computer syncs more history.** A new account on a computer now syncs six months of mail instead of three — phones and tablets stay at three, and either can be changed per account in Settings.
- **Newer assistants can connect to your mail.** Assistants built on the newest version of the Model Context Protocol can now connect to Allodia Mail & Calendar.

### 🔧 Fixed

- **Address suggestions stop pushing the composer around.** Address suggestions now float over the message instead of pushing the rest of the composer down.
- **Assistants no longer drop some mail tools.** Assistants that quietly skipped some of the mail tools, or ignored their optional settings, now read all of them correctly.
- **Folders open from the unified list.** Choosing a folder now opens that folder, even when All Inboxes is showing.
- **Moving one event of a series twice works.** Moving a single event of a repeating series a second time now changes that event, instead of failing or leaving the first move behind.

## 🐧 Linux (in development, not yet released)

### ✨ New

- **A new account on a computer syncs more history.** A new account on a computer now syncs six months of mail instead of three — phones and tablets stay at three, and either can be changed per account in Settings.
- **Linux calendar drag-to-create.** On Linux, drag across empty calendar grid space to draw a new event slot and open it in the editor.
- **Linux desktop integration and accessibility.** On Linux, mail links now open a pre-filled composer, screen readers can open folders and messages, and signature editing stays stable while assistive technology is running.
- **Linux feedback and calendar management.** On Linux, you can now see sync progress, manage calendar visibility, colours and defaults, keep calendars refreshed, export diagnostics, control notifications and analytics, and answer time-zone changes.
- **Linux gains local assistant access.** On Linux, Settings can now enable local MCP access, choose shared accounts and open assistant-created drafts for review without sending them.
- **Linux recovers Microsoft permissions.** On Linux, a missing Microsoft mail or calendar permission now offers Reconnect and asks for the full permission set again.
- **Linux repairs expired credentials.** On Linux, you can now renew an expired JMAP sign-in or replace a refused password or API token without removing the account.
- **Linux shows avatars.** Linux now shows sender and contact avatars in the message list, reading pane and contacts.
- **Linux shows connection problems.** On Linux, Allodia Mail & Calendar now shows when you are offline, flags accounts it cannot reach, and reports an expired sign-in separately from a server outage.
- **Marking spam teaches your provider.** Marking a message as spam now tells your mail provider, so its own filter learns from it, instead of only moving the message to Junk.
- **Newer assistants can connect to your mail.** Assistants built on the newest version of the Model Context Protocol can now connect to Allodia Mail & Calendar.

### 🔧 Fixed

- **A draft you are writing is not lost on Linux.** On Linux, opening another message while you are writing now asks before discarding your draft.
- **A missing Sent copy can be dismissed on Linux.** On Linux, a sent message whose copy could not be saved now offers Not now as well as Save to Sent.
- **An attachment says which file it belongs to.** On Linux, a screen reader now says which file an attachment's Open and Save belong to, and a file name containing an ampersand is read out as it was written.
- **An attendee is listed by name and address.** On Linux, an event's attendee list now shows each person's address under their name, instead of repeating the address of anyone who has no name.
- **Assistants no longer drop some mail tools.** Assistants that quietly skipped some of the mail tools, or ignored their optional settings, now read all of them correctly.
- **Folders open from the unified list.** Choosing a folder now opens that folder, even when All Inboxes is showing.
- **Linux MCP configuration selects its Flatpak installation.** On Linux, copied MCP configuration now selects the active Flatpak installation and starts without an interactive choice.
- **Linux avatar borders render cleanly.** Linux avatars no longer show a stray line between the initials and their border.
- **Linux calendar opens at the current time.** On Linux, the calendar now opens around the current time instead of starting at midnight.
- **Linux dialogs use one title.** On Linux, dialogs now use one native title bar instead of repeating their title inside the window.
- **Linux folder switching stays steady.** Switching folders on Linux is now immediate, without the selection flickering back to the previous folder.
- **Linux localizes mail dates.** On Linux, message lists now show compact relative dates, and an opened message shows its full date and time in your selected time zone.
- **The calendar stays where you left it.** Opening an appointment no longer scrolls the day back to midnight, so the calendar stays on the hours you were looking at.
- **The open message stays selected on Linux.** On Linux, an unread message now stays selected after you open it.
- **The usage-data choice can be reached with the keyboard.** On Linux, the usage-data choice on the welcome screen can now be reached with the keyboard, instead of only with a mouse.

The Linux app cannot be installed yet. The above is what landed this cycle, listed for completeness.
